Before reaching for any object, it helps to name the problem. You have a
handful of machines and a set of processes that must stay running across
reboots, crashes, and deploys. Doing that by hand does not scale past a few
hosts.

A <Term>cluster</Term> turns those machines into one pool of compute. You stop
addressing individual servers and start addressing the pool.

The part that does the deciding is the <Term>control plane</Term>. You hand it a
<Term>manifest</Term> describing what you want, and it works out how to get
there. That style is what makes Kubernetes <Term>declarative</Term>: you state
an end result rather than a sequence of commands.
