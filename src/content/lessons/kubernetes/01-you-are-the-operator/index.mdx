You are responsible for keeping a system running. Something has changed. Your
job is to make the system match its specification.

That is the whole brief. There is no Kubernetes in this lesson, no YAML, and
nothing to install. You get three things: a statement of what the system is
supposed to look like, a view of what it currently looks like, and a stream of
events telling you that things happened.

> **Interactive.** Seven levels of the operator game mount here. The engine and
> the UI are not written yet, so see the roadmap for the level design.

The levels get harder in a specific way. The first one is a server that
disappeared, and the fix is obvious. Then there is one server too many. Then an
event fires and the right move is to do nothing at all, because the system is
already correct. Then several events at once, all of them noise. Then the world
changes while you are still acting on it.

Somewhere in there, most people stop reading the events and start counting the
servers. That is what the lesson is for.

## What you just worked out

Reacting to events does not work, because an event tells you that something
happened rather than what the system needs. What works is a loop: look at the
<Term>desired state</Term>, look at the <Term>actual state</Term>, act on the
difference, then look again.

That loop is called <Term>reconciliation</Term>, and a thing that runs it is a
<Term>control loop</Term>. You have just spent ten minutes being one by hand.

Kubernetes is a large collection of these loops. Every lesson after this one is
either a loop, the state a loop reads, or the machinery that lets a loop act.
