A ReplicaSet counts pods, and it really doesn't care what they're called or where
they run. For most things that's exactly right. Two kinds of workload need one
of those to matter, and each one gets its own object.

## One on every node

A <Term>DaemonSet</Term> runs one pod on every node:

```yaml
kind: DaemonSet
metadata:
  name: logs
spec:
  selector:
    matchLabels:
      app: logs
  template:
    metadata:
      labels:
        app: logs
    spec:
      containers:
        - name: shipper
          image: shop/log-shipper:4.2
```

There's no `replicas`. The count is however many nodes you have, and it changes
when they do. Its controller walks the nodes, and any node without one of its
pods gets one, pinned to that node.

That's for stuff that's about the machine rather than your app: something
shipping the node's log files, a metrics agent reading its CPU and disk, the
network plugin that hands pods their addresses. Look in `kube-system` on pretty
much any cluster and you'll find a few you never installed.

Put a `nodeSelector` in the template and it only counts the nodes that match, so
a GPU driver installer runs on the GPU nodes and nowhere else.

DaemonSet pods also stay put where other pods get pushed off. `kubectl drain`
refuses to empty a node that has them unless you pass `--ignore-daemonsets`,
because evicting one is pointless, it'd just be put straight back on the same
node.

## Pods with names

A <Term>StatefulSet</Term> is basically a Deployment with two extra fields:

```yaml
kind: StatefulSet
metadata:
  name: db
spec:
  serviceName: db
  replicas: 3
  selector:
    matchLabels:
      app: db
  template:
    metadata:
      labels:
        app: db
    spec:
      containers:
        - name: postgres
          image: postgres:17
          volumeMounts:
            - name: data
              mountPath: /var/lib/postgresql/data
  volumeClaimTemplates:
    - metadata:
        name: data
      spec:
        accessModes: [ReadWriteOnce]
        resources:
          requests:
            storage: 20Gi
```

The pods aren't called `db-6c8f4b-k2r8s`. They're `db-0`, `db-1` and `db-2`, and
those names are the whole point. `db-0` is made first, and `db-1` doesn't start
until `db-0` is running and ready. Scaling down goes the other way, highest
number first. And when `db-1` goes away, the pod that replaces it is `db-1`
again.

`volumeClaimTemplates` gives each pod its own disk, named after it: `data-db-0`,
`data-db-1`, `data-db-2`. The disk belongs to the name rather than to the pod,
so a new `db-1` gets `data-db-1` back with everything that was on it. Scaling
down doesn't delete disks either. Take the set down to two and `data-db-2` just
sits there until a `db-2` comes back for it.

`serviceName` gives every pod a DNS name of its own, like `db-0.db`, and that's
why the stable names matter. A Postgres replica that's been told to follow
`db-0` needs `db-0` to still mean the same database tomorrow.

## Updating them

Both of them roll out when you change the template too, they just don't make
ReplicaSets to do it. They keep their old templates in ControllerRevision objects
instead, and that's what `kubectl rollout undo` goes back to.

By default a DaemonSet doesn't start the new pod next to the old one, since two
log shippers on one node would fight over the same files. It deletes the pod on
one node, waits for the replacement to be ready, and moves to the next. That's
`maxUnavailable: 1`, and you can raise it to go faster.

A StatefulSet updates one pod at a time, from the highest number down, waiting
for each to be ready before the next. Set `partition: 2` under `rollingUpdate`
and only pods numbered 2 and up get the new template, which makes `db-2` a
canary you can watch before letting the rest go.

## What it won't do for you

A StatefulSet gives a database stable names and disks that stick around. It
doesn't copy data between pods, pick a primary, or fail over when one dies.
That's still the database's job, and wiring it up is the hard part, which is why
most people run databases on top of something that already knows how, or just
outside the cluster.
